Sound rules from the videoThe stuff that isn't obvious

ä / ö / üthe Umlaute. Short ä ≈ a short English "e". ü = say "ee" with rounded lips.
chtwo versions: soft (ich, after e/i) and hard/throaty (machen, after a/o/u).
-igat the end of a word sounds like -ich → Honig = "Honich".
h after a vowelis silent and makes the vowel long: Sahne, Bahn.
ie= one long "ee": liegen. (But ei = "eye": mein.)
final rvocalised / barely spoken: Vater, Mutter — you hear "Vata", "Mutta".
v / wv = English "f" (vier); w = English "v" (Wasser). easy to swap by mistake!
ß"scharfes S" = sharp/voiceless s, and makes the vowel before it long: Straße, Spaß.
sch= English "sh". z = English "ts" (Zebra = "tsebra").
Diphthongs: ei → "eye", au → "ow", eu / äu → "oy". So Häuser = "HOY-za".
