73 · Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch

Both objects in one sentence. Someone gives 📋 a thing (Akkusativ) to 🧑‍💻 a person (Dativ) — and Herr Antrim’s video answers the one real question: which comes first? Two nouns: the Dativ. But an Akkusativ pronoun (es, ihn, sie) always comes first. You know all the pronouns now (lessons 66 and 70), so you can use the full rule.

🔁 RevisionFrom earlier lessons

The shapeDoer · receiver · thing

Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch.

👩‍💼 ich — the doer (Nominativ)
🧑‍💻 dem Mann — the receiver (Dativ)
📋 das Buch — the thing given (Akkusativ)

Verbs that work this way: geben (give), schenken (give as a gift), zeigen (show), schicken (send), erklären (explain), kaufen (buy for someone).

The order ruleTwo nouns → Dativ first

When both objects are nouns, the receiver comes first:

Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch. ✓  (Dativ → Akkusativ)

English has the same shape: I give the man the book. So the order will already feel natural — it is the endings that are new, not the shape.

The exceptionAn Akkusativ pronoun comes first

As soon as the thing becomes a pronoun (es, ihn, sie), it jumps in front of the receiver — whether the receiver is a noun or a pronoun:

Ich gebe es dem Mann. ✓  (not Ich gebe dem Mann es)
Ich gebe es ihm. ✓  (not Ich gebe ihm es)

A Dativ pronoun changes nothing, because the Dativ is first anyway:
Ich gebe ihm das Buch. ✓

The whole rule in one line: Dativ before Akkusativ — unless the Akkusativ is a pronoun; then the Akkusativ goes first.
Watch the gender of the pronoun. The pronoun takes the gender of the noun it replaces (lesson 66):
den Brief → ihn:  Ich schicke ihn der Frau.
die Schokolade → sie:  Ich schenke sie dem Kind.
das Foto → es:  Ich zeige es ihr.
Bonus — the order that is not wrong, just marked: Ich gebe das Buch dem Mann (with the stress on dem Mann) is possible German — it means to the man, not to someone else. The neutral, everyday order is Dativ first; use that.
