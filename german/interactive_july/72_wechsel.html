72 · Die Wechselpräpositionen

Nine prepositions that take either case, depending on meaning. You met these back in lesson 35 — but now you have both cases properly, so this time the rule rests on something. We will also be honest about where the usual shortcut breaks down.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

The nineThe ones that can go either way

an · auf · hinter · in · neben · über · unter · vor · zwischen

All nine are about position in space. Every one of them can take either the Dativ or the Akkusativ, and the case changes the meaning.

The ruleChange of location, or not?

No change of location → Dativ. The thing is somewhere, and it stays there.
Der Stift liegt auf dem Tisch. — the pen is lying on the table

Change of location → Akkusativ. The thing crosses into a new place.
Er legt den Stift auf den Tisch. — he puts the pen onto the table

The video drills exactly this pairing: die Schüler sitzen in dem Bus vs die Schüler gehen in den Bus. Same preposition, same bus — the case tells you whether they are riding or boarding.
Now the honest part — and this is where lesson 35 was too confident. You will be taught the shortcut ‘wo? → Dativ, wohin? → Akkusativ’, and it is a useful heuristic, not a law. Textbooks often present it as ‘the whole game’. It is not, and here is the proof:

Ich bin die ganze Zeit in der Stadt herumgefahren.
Plenty of movement — driving around for hours — yet Dativ. Why? Because nothing changed location: the driving happened inside the city the whole time.

So the real test is not “is something moving?” but “does something cross from one place into another?” Movement within a space keeps the Dativ.
Bonus — the same preposition, two readings:
Wir kamen in das Dorf. — we came into the village (crossing in)
Wir kamen in dem Dorf an. — we arrived in the village (the arriving happened there)

English has no wo/wohin split at all, so there is no instinct from your first language to lean on here. That is why this one needs conscious attention for a while.

ContractionsThe short forms

an + dem = amam Strand — location (Dativ)
an + das = ansans Meer — going there (Akkusativ)
in + dem = imim Wald — location (Dativ)
in + das = insins Kino — going there (Akkusativ)
auf + das = aufsaufs Land — going there (Akkusativ)
