71 · Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch

Now both cases in one sentence — the moment the company metaphor pays off. Someone gives 📋 a thing to 🧑‍💻 a person. The thing is Akkusativ, the person is Dativ, and there is a simple rule for which comes first.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

The shapeDoer · receiver · thing

Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch.

👩‍💼 ich — the doer (Nominativ)
🧑‍💻 dem Mann — the receiver (Dativ)
📋 das Buch — the thing given (Akkusativ)

Verbs that work this way: geben (give), schenken (give as a gift), zeigen (show), schicken (send), erklären (explain), kaufen (buy for someone).

The order ruleTwo nouns → Dativ first

When both objects are nouns, the receiver comes first:

Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch. ✓  (Dativ → Akkusativ)

English does the same thing without any endings: I give the man the book. So the order will already feel natural — it is the endings that are new, not the shape.
But a pronoun jumps to the front. As soon as one object becomes a short word like es or ihn, it overtakes:

Ich gebe es dem Mann. ✓  (not Ich gebe dem Mann es)
Ich gebe es ihm. ✓  (both pronouns: Akkusativ first!)

The habit to build: short words move left. You will meet ihm and ihn properly in the next two lessons — for now just notice that pronouns behave differently.
