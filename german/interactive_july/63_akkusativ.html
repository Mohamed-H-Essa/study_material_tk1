63 · Der Akkusativ

Your first real case change — and it is smaller than you think. Of the four article forms, exactly one changes: der → den. Feminine, neuter and plural do not move at all. You already know the genders, so three quarters of this lesson is free.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

The whole ruleder → den. That is it.

The Akkusativ is the direct object — the task 📋, the thing the verb is done to. Here is the entire change:

der Hund → Ich sehe den Hund.  ⬅ the only one that moves
die Katze → Ich sehe die Katze.  unchanged
das Kind → Ich sehe das Kind.  unchanged
die Kinder → Ich sehe die Kinder.  unchanged
Do not file this as “the accusative usually does nothing”. That is the classic wrong lesson to draw, and it makes people drop the one form that matters. der is the most common article in German — masculine nouns are everywhere — so in real speech this change fires constantly. Think of it as: one article changes, and it happens to be the busiest one.

Finding itThe question wen oder was?

The video says it directly: der Akkusativ antwortet auf die Frage wen oder was?

Ich sehe den Mülleimer. → Wen oder was sehe ich? → den Mülleimer

Ask wer? to find the doer, wen? to find the thing it is done to.

VerbsVerbs that take a direct object

These come straight from the video, and they are among the most common verbs in the language: haben (to have), brauchen (to need), sehen (to see), lieben (to love), hassen (to hate). Each one asks ‘wen oder was?’ — so each one puts a masculine noun into den.
