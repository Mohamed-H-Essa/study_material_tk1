70 · Verben mit Dativ

A small group of very common verbs take the Dativ where English would use a plain object. Ich helfe dem Mann, not den Mann. There is no rule to work out — these are learned one by one, and they are worth it because you use them daily.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

The ideaEnglish says “help someone”, German says “help TO someone”

With most verbs the object takes the Akkusativ. But a handful take the Dativ instead — because in German logic, the person is receiving the helping, the thanking, the pleasing:

Ich helfe dem Mann. — I help the man
Ich danke der Frau. — I thank the woman
Das gehört dem Kind. — that belongs to the child

The listThe ones you will actually use

—helfento help — Ich helfe dem Mann.
—dankento thank — Ich danke der Frau.
—gehörento belong to — Das gehört dem Kind.
—gefallento please / to like — Das gefällt mir.
—schmeckento taste good — Das schmeckt mir.
—passento fit / suit — Das passt mir.
—antwortento answer (a person) — Ich antworte dem Lehrer.
—folgento follow — Ich folge dem Auto.

The big onegefallen — German says it backwards

gefallen flips the sentence compared to English. The thing is the subject, and the person receives the pleasing:

Der Film gefällt mir. — literally the film pleases to-me = I like the film
Die Filme gefallen mir. — plural subject, so the verb changes: gefallen

So the thing you like is in the Nominativ, and you are in the Dativ. Same shape as schmecken (Das schmeckt mir = I like the taste) and passen.
