67 · Präpositionen mit Akkusativ

Herr Antrim's video lists the prepositions that take the Akkusativ. Five of them are everyday A1 words that always take it — durch, für, gegen, ohne, um — so after them you never decide anything: den, einen, meinen, mich, ihn, exactly as in lessons 64–66. The video's three extras (bis, wider, entlang) are explained honestly at the end: one useful, one old-fashioned, one tricky.

The setFive words, always Akkusativ

durch — through  · Wir gehen durch den Park.
für — for  · Das Geschenk ist für meinen Vater.
gegen — against  · Wir laufen gegen den Wind.
ohne — without  · Ich gehe nicht ohne dich.
um — around · at (clock time)  · Wir sitzen um den Tisch. · um acht Uhr
Same pattern as lessons 64–66. The preposition simply puts the next noun or pronoun into the Akkusativ — and, as always, among the der/ein-words and er/sie/es only the masculine shows it; ich, du, wir, ihr use their object forms from lesson 66:

für den Vater · für einen Freund · für meinen Bruder · für ihn
für die Mutter · für das Kind · für meine Eltern · für sie
für mich · für dich · für uns · für euch
The mnemonic. Say them in this order until it is a single word: durch-für-gegen-ohne-um. One breath. English-speaking teachers also use the acronym DOGFU (durch, ohne, gegen, für, um) — pick whichever sticks. When any of these five appears, you stop deciding: it is Akkusativ, always.
Why a chant beats a rule here. These prepositions never change their case — unlike the two-way ones you will meet in lesson 74. So there is genuinely nothing to work out. Chanting a closed set is the cheapest way to make it automatic, and it frees your attention for the parts that do need thought.

The video's extrasbis, wider, entlang — the honest version

bonus bis (until, as far as) is a useful everyday word and does take the Akkusativ — but you rarely see it, because it usually stands without an article: bis Montag, bis morgen, bis Berlin. When you do see it: bis nächsten Freitag. With an article German normally adds a second preposition, and then that one decides the case (bis zum Bahnhof — lesson 71). Learn bis as a word; do not add it to the chant.
bonus wider (against) is old-fashioned and literary. You will meet it only in fixed phrases like wider Willen (against one's will). In everyday German say gegen. Do not confuse it with wieder (again) — same sound, different word.
bonus entlang (along) is tricky: it normally comes after the noun, and then it takes the Akkusativ: Wir gehen den Fluss entlang. Placed before the noun it takes a different case (Dativ or Genitiv), so for now use only the pattern den … entlang.
