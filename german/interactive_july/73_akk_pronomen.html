73 · Personalpronomen im Akkusativ

Pronouns change for case too — and here English actually helps a little, because it does the same thing: I → me, he → him. German just has more forms and keeps them tidier.

The formsNominativ → Akkusativ

ich → michI → me
du → dichyou → you
er → ihnhe → him
sie → sieshe → her (no change)
es → esit → it (no change)
wir → unswe → us
ihr → euchyou (pl.) → you
sie → siethey → them (no change)
Sie → Sieyou (formal) → you (no change)
Notice the same pattern as the articles. The masculine is where the visible change happens: er → ihn, just like der → den. Feminine, neuter and plural stay put — sie, es, sie. Half of this table is already free.
Do not mix up sie and Sie. Lower-case sie = she or they; capital Sie = you (polite). All three are unchanged in the Akkusativ, so context and the capital letter are your only clues — which is fine, because in speech the verb ending usually settles it.
