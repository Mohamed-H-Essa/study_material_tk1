47 · Die Prinzessin und der Wirt

A German legend: a princess falls in love with a servant, they run away and open an inn, and the emperor — recognising his daughter's cooking — makes the innkeeper a count. That is how Baden-Württemberg supposedly got its name: der Wirt am Berg.

This story is told in the simple past (Präteritum), the tense German uses for written narrative: lebte (lived), war (was), hatte (had), sagte (said), machte (made), akzeptierte (accepted). Regular verbs form it with -te: leben → lebte, sagen → sagte.
sich verlieben in + accusative: Die Prinzessin verliebt sich in einen Diener. Another fixed verb+preposition pair to learn as a unit. Compare verliebt sein (to be in love) and verlobt (engaged) and verheiratet (married).
Comparing two people with so … wie: Keiner kann das Essen so gut kochen wie die Prinzessin. = nobody can cook it as well as the princess. Use so … wie for equality and -er … als for difference — never mix them.
The pun the whole legend rests on: Du bist der Wirt am Berg → Württemberg. The emperor names him after his inn on the hill. Whether or not it is true history, it is how the story is told.
Two useful separables here: Der Kaiser zieht weg. (moves away) · Er kommt später zurück. (comes back). And a dative verb: Wir wollten dir nicht wehtun. = we didn't want to hurt you — wehtun always takes the dative.
