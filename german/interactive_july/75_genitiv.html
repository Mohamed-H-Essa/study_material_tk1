75 · Der Genitiv

The fourth and last case: the owner 🏢. Following the video, you meet the Genitiv articles des · der · des · der, the -s / -es that masculine and neuter nouns pick up (das Auto des Vaters), the question wessen? (whose?), and the -s on names you have used since lesson 19. The goal today is recognition first — read it confidently — plus a little production, and the honest note that in speech Germans often say von + Dativ instead.

🔁 RevisionFrom earlier lessons

Start hereYou already use one kind

You have been reading a genitive since lesson 19 without knowing it: Emmas Familie, Emmas Bruder, Tinas Familie. With a name, German just adds -s — no apostrophe:

Ottos Haus · Emmas Woche · Mustafas Auto

That is the owner 🏢 — the fourth case — and you already have it.
bonus A name that already ends in an s-sound (-s, -ß, -x, -z) takes just an apostrophe instead: Klaus' Auto, Max' Zimmer.

The formsdes · der · des · der

Masculine der Mann → das Auto des Mannes
Neuter das Kind → das Zimmer des Kindes
Feminine die Frau → die Tasche der Frau
Plural die Kinder → die Spielsachen der Kinder

Two pairs: masculine and neuter share des (just as they share dem in the Dativ), and feminine and plural share der.
The noun changes too — but only masculine and neuter. After des the noun adds -s or -es:

-es — mostly short, one-syllable nouns: des Mannes, des Kindes, des Tisches; and always after -s, -ß, -x, -z: des Hauses
-s — longer nouns: des Vaters, des Lehrers, des Autos, des Zimmers

Feminine and plural nouns take no ending: der Frau, der Kinder. So des + -(e)s is a double signal you cannot miss when reading.

bonus A small group of masculine nouns take -(e)n instead of -s: des Jungen, des Kollegen, des Menschen (the n-declension — a later topic).
Two word-order points. The Genitiv comes after the thing owned — the opposite of English the man's car: das Auto des Mannes (literally the car of the man). Only names go in front: Emmas Auto.

And watch der once more: in die Tasche der Frau it is feminine Genitiv, in die Spielsachen der Kinder it is plural Genitiv. Read the position — der right after another noun usually means of the.

The questionwessen? — whose?

Four questions, four cases: wer? (Nom) · wen? (Akk) · wem? (Dat) · wessen? (Gen).

Wessen Auto ist das? — Das ist das Auto des Vaters.
Wessen Tasche ist das? — Das ist die Tasche der Frau.

Like the names, wessen stands directly in front of the noun it asks about.

Real lifeIn speech: von + Dativ

What you will actually hear. The Genitiv is everywhere in writing — news, signs, books. In everyday speech, though, Germans very often use von + Dativ instead, and you already know both halves (the Dativ from lesson 69, von and vom from lesson 71):

das Auto des Vaters → das Auto vom Vater
die Tasche der Frau → die Tasche von der Frau
das Haus meines Vaters → das Haus von meinem Vater

Both are correct. So: recognise des / der + -(e)s when you read, use -s on names, and say von + Dativ whenever the Genitiv does not come to you yet.
bonus Preview: a few prepositions take the Genitiv — wegen (because of), trotz (despite), während (during): wegen des Wetters. That is a later topic; just recognise them for now.
