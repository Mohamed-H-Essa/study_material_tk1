69 · Der Dativ

You already use this case correctly — you just did not know its name. vor dem Haus, mit dem Bus, in der Küche: every one of those is Dativ. This lesson, following the video, names the four Dativ articles — dem · der · dem · den — gives you the question that finds the Dativ, wem?, and adds the one extra detail of the plural: the noun takes an -n too (mit den Kindern).

Start hereYou already own a piece of this

If vor dem Haus and vor der Tür already sound right to you, you have absorbed the Dativ from listening. That is a good way to meet it: many courses teach chunks like in der Cafeteria or mit dem Auto long before they name the case. You got there by ear. Now we make it systematic.

The formsdem · der · dem · den

Masculine der Mann → dem Mann
Feminine die Frau → der Frau
Neuter das Kind → dem Kind
Plural die Kinder → den Kindern

Two patterns make it easy: masculine and neuter are both dem (an m at the end), and in the plural the noun takes an extra -n as well — more on that below.
The one that catches everybody: feminine die becomes der in the Dativ. Yes — der, the word you learned as the masculine article. die Frau → mit der Frau. This is exactly why storing der = the hurts later: here der is feminine. It is a job label, not a translation. You already do this correctly in vor der Tür — trust that instinct.

The meaningWho receives it — wem?

The Dativ is the indirect object — the employee 🧑‍💻 who receives something. Find it with wem? (to whom?):

Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch. → Wem gebe ich das Buch? → dem Mann

Three questions now: wer? → subject · wen? → direct object · wem? → receiver.

The pluralden + an extra -n on the noun

In the Dativ plural, the noun itself takes an extra -n — not just the article:

die Kinder → mit den Kindern
die Freunde → mit den Freunden
die Männer → mit den Männern

If the plural already ends in -n or -s, nothing is added: mit den Frauen, mit den Autos.
Why this one gets dropped. It carries no new information — den already told you it is Dativ plural, so the -n is pure redundancy. Your brain skips redundant endings by default (the same reason the cases were invisible to you before). It is a small detail, but it is one of the clearest markers of a careful speaker, so listen for it.
