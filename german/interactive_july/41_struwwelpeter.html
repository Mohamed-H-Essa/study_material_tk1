41 · Die Geschichte vom bösen Friedrich

A famous (and famously grim) German children's book from 1845. Wicked Frederick torments animals and people until the dog bites back. Dark, but culturally essential — every German knows der Struwwelpeter.

The moral arc is all about who does what to whom, so watch the cases. Friedrich schlägt den Hund. (accusative — the dog is the object) then it flips: Der Hund beißt Friedrich ins Bein. (the dog is now the subject).
jemandem etwas tun — dative for the person, accusative for the thing. Er reißt den Fliegen (dat.) die Flügel (acc.) aus. = he tears the wings off the flies. This dative-of-the-affected-person shows up constantly in German.
Body parts take the definite article, not a possessive. Der Hund beißt Friedrich ins Bein. — literally "bites Friedrich into the leg", where English says "his leg". Same in Das Bein blutet.
Pain and thirst are things you have: Der Hund hat Schmerzen. · Friedrich hat Schmerzen im Bein. · Der Hund hat Hunger und Durst. Same haben pattern as Hunger haben.
Ending on a contrast with und: Jetzt geht es dem Hund gut und Friedrich schlecht. Note es geht jemandem gut/schlecht — "someone is doing well/badly" — always with the dative. This is the standard way to say how a person is.
