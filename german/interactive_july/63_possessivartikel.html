63 · Mein Haus, meine Familie

Whose is it? The video walks one noun of each kind — das Haus, die Familie, der Arzt, die Stühle — through every owner: mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr, Ihr. Everything here is Nominativ (the subject, or after sein), so there are only two shapes to learn: mein and meine. Then you practise the one thing English speakers get wrong: sein or ihr?

The patternTwo shapes: mein or meine

The video's table, in the Nominativ. Masculine and neuter nouns take the bare form; feminine and plural nouns add -e. You already know this pattern from ein Haus · ein Arzt · eine Familie — the possessives follow ein exactly (and, unlike ein, they also have a plural: meine Stühle).

Two small things to notice in the table:

1. ihr means her and their, and with a capital Ihr it is the formal your. Context decides: Anna und ihr Haus (her), die Kinder und ihr Haus (their), Herr Müller, ist das Ihr Haus? (your). At the start of a sentence the capital cannot help you — Ihre Familie wohnt in Bayern could be her, their or your family; the video means her.

2. euer loses its middle e as soon as an ending is added: euer Haus, euer Arzt — but eure Familie, eure Stühle. (unser keeps it: unsere.)

The key confusionsein or ihr? Two separate decisions

English speakers see seine with its -e and think “that must be her”. It is not. Every possessive answers two questions, and they are decided by different nouns:

1. The OWNER decides the word. A man or a masculine/neuter thing owns it → sein. A woman, a feminine thing or a group (they) owns it → ihr.
2. The NOUN after it decides the ending. Masculine/neuter → no ending. Feminine/plural → -e.

Tom und seine Schwester — Tom owns (→ sein), die Schwester is feminine (→ -e).
Lisa und ihr Bruder — Lisa owns (→ ihr), der Bruder is masculine (→ no ending).

So seine Familie = his family, and ihr Arzt = her doctor. The ending tells you about the family or the doctor, never about the owner.
preview Everything in this lesson is Nominativ — the possessive is the subject (Mein Arzt ist nett) or sits after sein (Das ist mein Arzt). When a masculine noun becomes the object, mein will change to meinen — you meet that in lesson 65. Don't use it yet.
