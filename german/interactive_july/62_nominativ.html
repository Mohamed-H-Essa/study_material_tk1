62 · Der Nominativ

Jenny's video teaches the subject pronouns — ich, du, er, sie, es, wir, ihr, sie, Sie — and the verb ending that goes with each one (ich arbeite, du arbeitest …). These pronouns are the Nominativ: the doer form, the boss 👩‍💼 from lesson 60. You also learn to tell the three sies apart, to find the subject with wer?, and why sein keeps the Nominativ on both sides.

🔁 RevisionFrom earlier lessons

The easy partYou have known the Nominativ since lesson 1

The Nominativ is the subject — the one doing the verb. For nouns, its articles are exactly the dictionary forms you already have:

der Mann · die Frau · das Kind · die Kinder (plural)

Every noun you learned in Stufe 2 was taught in the Nominativ. What is new today is the other kind of subject: the pronoun.

In sentencesPart 1 · ich, du, er … — the pronouns with arbeiten

Ich arbeite. Du arbeitest.

I work. You work.

ich = Idu = you (one person, informal: family, friends, partner, children)

Er arbeitet. Sie arbeitet. Es arbeitet.

He works. She works. It works.

er = hesie = shees = it

Wir arbeiten. Ihr arbeitet.

We work. You (all) work.

wir = weihr = you all (informal plural: friends, kids, family)

sie arbeiten – Sie arbeiten

they work – you work (formal)

sie = theySie = you (formal, always a capital S: strangers, officials, a doctor, people you have never met)

The endings, all on one screen. The video's point: the pronoun and the verb ending belong together.

ich arbeite · du arbeitest · er / sie / es arbeitet
wir arbeiten · ihr arbeitet · sie / Sie arbeiten

The normal endings are -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en (ich spiele, du spielst, er spielt …). arbeiten adds an extra e before -st and -t only because its stem already ends in t — try saying arbeitst.

In sentencesPart 2 · sie, sie oder Sie? — telling them apart

Wo ist Anna? – Sie arbeitet.

Where is Anna? – She's working. bonus

sie + -t = she

Wo sind Tom und Lisa? – Sie arbeiten.

Where are Tom and Lisa? – They're working. bonus

sie + -en = they

Frau Weber, arbeiten Sie hier?

Ms Weber, do you work here? bonus

Sie + -en, capital S = you (formal)

Wo ist der Tisch? – Er ist in der Küche.

Where is the table? – It's in the kitchen. bonus

der Tisch · die Tische → er (a thing, but masculine)

Wer arbeitet hier? – Ich arbeite hier.

Who works here? – I work here. bonus

wer? = who? (finds the subject)

Three sies — read the verb, not the pronoun.

sie arbeitet → singular ending → she
sie arbeiten → plural ending → they
Sie arbeiten → plural ending + capital S → you (formal)

At the start of a sentence every Sie has a capital, so they and formal you look identical — then only context decides (Frau Weber, … tells you it is you).
Things get er / sie / es too. German pronouns follow the gender, not “person or thing”: der Tisch → er, die Lampe → sie, das Buch → es. English says it for all three; German does not. This is one more reason why you learned every noun with its article.

Finding itThe question wer?

To find the subject, ask wer? (who?) or was? (what?) with the verb:

Der Hund schläft. → Wer schläft? → der Hund → er
Die Kinder spielen. → Wer spielt? → die Kinder → sie

Note: wer itself always takes the er-ending (Wer spielt?), even when the answer is plural.

The one surprisesein takes the Nominativ on BOTH sides

You saw in lessons 60–61 that an object can change its label (der → den). sein (to be) does not do that. It is an equals sign, so both sides stay Nominativ — the same is true of werden (to become) and heißen (to be called):

Das ist der Lehrer. ✓  (never den Lehrer)
Der Mann ist der Chef. ✓
Tom wird der neue Chef. ✓

Compare: Ich sehe den Lehrer. — sehen is done to the teacher, so he gets the object label (full story in lesson 64). If you can replace the verb with ‘=’, keep the Nominativ.
