62 · Der Nominativ

The good news first: you already know the whole Nominativ. It is the form every word was taught to you in — der Tisch, die Lampe, das Kind. This lesson just gives it a name and shows you the one place it surprises people.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

The easy partYou have known this since lesson 1

The Nominativ is the subject — the boss 👩‍💼, the one doing the verb. Its articles are exactly the dictionary forms you already have:

der Mann · die Frau · das Kind · die Kinder (plural)

Every single noun you learned in Stufe 2 was taught in the Nominativ. Nothing new to memorise here.

Finding itThe question wer?

To find the subject, ask wer? (who?) or was? (what?) in front of the verb:

Der Hund schläft. → Wer schläft? → der Hund ✓
Die Kinder spielen. → Wer spielt? → die Kinder ✓

The one surprisesein, heißen, werden take Nominativ on BOTH sides

Most verbs push the second noun into the Akkusativ. But sein (to be), heißen (to be called) and werden (to become) do not. They are an equals sign, so both sides stay Nominativ:

Das ist der Lehrer. ✓  (not den Lehrer)
Er heißt der Chef. ✓
Mein Bruder wird ein Arzt. ✓

Compare: Ich sehe den Lehrer. — sehen is a normal verb, so it takes the Akkusativ. If you can replace the verb with ‘=’, keep Nominativ.
