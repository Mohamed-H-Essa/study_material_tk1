61 · Wortstellung oder Endung?

The single idea that makes German cases make sense. English tells you who did what by where the words sit. German tells you by how the words are dressed. Once you see this, cases stop being decoration and become information.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

The core ideaEnglish moves words. German dresses them.

In English, position is the meaning. Watch what happens when nothing changes except the order:

The man sees the dog.
The dog sees the man.

Same five words. Opposite meaning. Nothing about the words themselves changed — only where they stand. English has almost no endings left, so word order has to carry the whole load.
German does it the other way round. The words carry a label, so they can move without losing their job:

Der Mann sieht den Hund. — the man sees the dog
Den Hund sieht der Mann. — still: the man sees the dog

The dog moved to the front, but it is still wearing den, so it is still the one being seen. German moved a word for emphasis and lost nothing, because the ending did the work.
This is the trap your brain will fall into — it is normal and it has a name. Learners automatically read the first noun as the doer. So Den Hund sieht der Mann feels like the dog sees the man. It is not. Researchers who tested this found learners scoring below 50% on sentences like these — worse than guessing. Not because they are bad at German, but because the English habit is very strong. The drills below exist to break exactly this habit, so expect them to feel awkward at first. That means they are working.

Why it matters to youWhy 59 lessons of listening did not give you this

You have understood hundreds of German sentences without ever needing den. That is not a failure — it is efficient. If you hear ich sehe den Hund, the meaning is obvious from the words alone, so your brain skips the ending. It only starts paying attention when the ending is the only thing that tells you the answer. That is what these drills do: they make the ending necessary.
