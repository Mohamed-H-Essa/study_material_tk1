75 · Dativ Plural und der Genitiv

Two loose ends. First a tiny rule that native speakers never miss and learners always do — the extra -n in the dative plural. Then a first look at the fourth case, which you can already read more of than you think.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

Part 1The dative plural adds -n

In the dative plural, the noun itself takes an extra -n — not just the article:

die Kinder → mit den Kindern
die Freunde → mit den Freunden
die Bücher → in den Büchern

If the plural already ends in -n or -s, nothing is added: mit den Frauen, mit den Autos.
Why this one gets dropped. It carries no new information — den already told you it is dative plural, so the -n is pure redundancy. Your brain skips redundant endings by default (the same reason the cases were invisible to you before). It is a small detail, but it is one of the clearest markers of a careful speaker, so it is worth the two minutes.

Part 2The Genitiv — you already use one kind

You have been reading a genitive since lesson 19 without knowing it: Emmas Familie, Emmas Bruder, Tinas Familie. With a name, German just adds -s — no apostrophe:

Ottos Haus · Emmas Woche · Mustafas Auto

That is the owner 🏢 — the fourth case — and you already have it.
With an article, the genitive looks like this:

Masculine der Mann → des Mannes
Neuter das Kind → des Kindes
Feminine die Frau → der Frau
Plural die Kinder → der Kinder

Masculine and neuter add -es (or just -s on longer words) to the noun as well: das Auto des Mannes — the man's car.
Read it, don't force it. This is a B1 topic — two levels above where you are now, and all three major course series (Menschen, Schritte, Nicos Weg) put it there. In everyday speech Germans overwhelmingly use von + Dativ instead:

das Auto von dem Mann (or vom Mann) — much more common in speech
das Auto des Mannes — correct, but formal/written

So: learn to recognise des and der when you read them, keep using -s on names, and say von + Dativ when you speak. When the genitive does properly arrive at B1, it will come through prepositions like wegen and trotz, not through possession.
