Everything in one place. The full table is here for reference — but do not try to memorise it as a grid. You already know this as a set of habits, and habits are what you will actually use when speaking.

ReferenceThe complete table

Nominativder · die · das · die — the doer 👩‍💼 (wer?)
Akkusativden · die · das · die — the thing 📋 (wen oder was?)
Dativdem · der · dem · den+n — the receiver 🧑‍💻 (wem?)
Genitivdes+es · der · des+es · der — the owner 🏢 (wessen?)
Do not learn this table by heart. Presented as a 4×4 grid it is sixteen disconnected facts, and research on learners is clear that memorising it does not transfer into speech. What actually works is what you have been doing for fifteen lessons: one rule at a time, drilled until it is automatic. Keep the table for looking things up, not for studying.

What to actually carryThe five habits that do the work

1. der → den when it is the thing being acted on. The only visible accusative change — and the busiest article in German.

2. durch für gegen ohne um → always Akkusativ.

3. aus außer bei mit nach seit von zu → always Dativ.

4. Feminine die → der in the Dativ. The one that feels wrong and is right.

5. Check the article before deciding who did it. Never trust word order alone.

Honest expectationsHow long this takes

Research on German learners is consistent and worth hearing: case accuracy arrives years after the topic is taught, and persistent errors for a long time are completely normal — not a sign the method failed.

So measure yourself the right way. Success right now is “I notice den when I read and hear it” — not “I never make a mistake”. Noticing comes first; accuracy follows on its own, from all the input you keep getting. Keep watching the videos. The cases will now stick to them.
