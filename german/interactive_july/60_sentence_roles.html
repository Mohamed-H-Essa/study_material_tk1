Start hereWhy this lesson has no German endings in it

You already know about 600 words and you know der / die / das for most of them. That is the hard part, and it is done. What you are missing is not vocabulary — it is a way to say what each word is doing in the sentence. That is all a “case” is.
The one mistake that costs people years: thinking der simply means the. It does not. der means the + masculine + this word is the one doing the action. When the job changes, the word changes. If you store der = the, every case later feels random. Store it as a job label instead. (der even has a second job with die-words — you meet it in lesson 69 — one more reason it can never be plain the.)

The metaphorA sentence is a small company

This is the picture Easy German uses in the video, and we will keep it for the whole Stufe. Every sentence is a little company:

👩‍💼 The boss — she decides and acts. Nominativ
📋 The task — the work that gets done. Akkusativ
🧑‍💻 The employee — the one who receives the task. Dativ
🏢 The owner — rarely around, but he owns the place. Genitiv

Four roles, four cases. That is the entire system. You do not need the names or the endings yet — each gets its own lesson.

In sentencesPart 1 · Die Firma — the four roles

Im Deutschen gibt es vier Fälle: Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ und Genitiv.

In German there are four cases: nominative, accusative, dative and genitive.

der Fall · die Fälle = case (grammar)

Stellt euch einen deutschen Satz so vor wie eine Firma.

Picture a German sentence like a company.

der Satz · die Sätze = sentencedie Firma · die Firmen = company, firm

Da gibt es zum Beispiel die Chefin. Das ist unser Nominativ.

There is, for example, the boss. That is our nominative.

die Chefin · die Chefinnen = (female) boss

Es gibt Projekte und verschiedene Aufgaben. Das ist unser Akkusativ.

There are projects and various tasks. That is our accusative.

das Projekt · die Projekte = projectdie Aufgabe · die Aufgaben = task

Das ist der motivierte Mitarbeiter. Er bekommt die Aufgaben. Das ist unser Dativ.

That is the motivated employee. He gets the tasks. That is our dative.

der Mitarbeiter · die Mitarbeiter = employee, colleague

Der Besitzer ist selten da, aber er ist trotzdem wichtig. Das ist unser Genitiv.

The owner is rarely there, but he is still important. That is our genitive.

der Besitzer · die Besitzer = owner

In sentencesPart 2 · Die Geschichte — the story

Als Chefin denke ich viel nach.

As the boss, I think things over a lot.

nachdenken (trennbar) = to think (something) over

Erik, endlich bekommst du ein neues Projekt.

Erik, you're finally getting a new project.

bekommen = to get, to receive (not “to become”!)

Der Mitarbeiter liebt neue Projekte, aber er hat zu viel Arbeit.

The employee loves new projects, but he has too much work.

die Arbeit (kein Plural) = work

Das Subjekt in einem Satz steht fast immer im Nominativ.

The subject of a sentence is almost always in the nominative.

das Subjekt · die Subjekte = subject

Fast jedes Verb braucht ein Subjekt im Nominativ.

Almost every verb needs a subject in the nominative.

das Verb · die Verben = verb

Word 1The verb — the action

The verb is the doing-word. In The man sees the dog, the verb is sees. In a German main clause it is the second thing in the sentence (you know this from Stufe 3): Der Mann sieht den Hund. Find the verb first, always. Everything else is defined relative to it.

Word 2The subject — who is doing it

Ask: who or what is doing the verb? That is the subject. The man sees the dog → who is doing the seeing? The man. The man is the subject. This is the boss 👩‍💼. In German the subject is in the Nominativ — which is the form you already learned when you learned the word: der Mann, die Frau, das Kind. (Lesson 62.)

Word 3The object — who it is done to

Ask: whom or what is the verb done to? That is the object. The man sees the dog → he sees whom? The dog. The dog is the object. This is the task 📋. In German the object goes into the Akkusativ — you will learn its forms in lesson 64. For now just notice that der Hund turned into den Hund in the example above.
Same word, different job. Look at these two English sentences:

The dog sees the man. → the dog is the subject (it is doing the seeing)
The man sees the dog. → the dog is the object (it is being seen)

Identical word, opposite job. English shows the difference by position. German can show it by changing the article. That is the whole idea, and lesson 61 is about exactly this.
