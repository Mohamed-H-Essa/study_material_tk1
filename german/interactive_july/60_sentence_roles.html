60 · Wer macht was?

The start of the cases — and we do not touch a single ending yet. First you need three words: verb, subject, object. Learn these three and the four cases stop being magic. Skip them and no amount of tables will help.

rainbow = bonus beyond the video

Start hereWhy this lesson has no German endings in it

You already know about 600 words and you know der / die / das for most of them. That is the hard part, and it is done. What you are missing is not vocabulary — it is a way to say what each word is doing in the sentence. That is all a “case” is.
The one mistake that costs people years: thinking der simply means the. It does not. der means the + masculine + this word is the one doing the action. When the job changes, the word changes: der → den. If you store der = the, every case later feels random. Store it as a job label instead.

The metaphorA sentence is a small company

This is the picture Easy German uses in the video, and we will keep it for the whole Stufe. Every sentence is a little company:

👩‍💼 The boss — she decides and acts. Nominativ
📋 The task — the work that gets done. Akkusativ
🧑‍💻 The employee — the one who receives the task. Dativ
🏢 The owner — rarely around, but he owns the place. Genitiv

Four roles, four cases. That is the entire system. You do not need the names yet.

Word 1The verb — the action

The verb is the doing-word. In The man sees the dog, the verb is sees. In German it is nearly always the second thing in the sentence: Der Mann sieht den Hund. Find the verb first, always. Everything else is defined relative to it.

Word 2The subject — who is doing it

Ask: who or what is doing the verb? That is the subject. The man sees the dog → who is doing the seeing? The man. The man is the subject. This is the boss 👩‍💼. In German the subject is in the Nominativ — which is the form you already learned when you learned the word: der Mann, die Frau, das Kind.

Word 3The object — who it is done to

Ask: whom or what is the verb done to? That is the object. The man sees the dog → he sees whom? The dog. The dog is the object. This is the task 📋. In German the object goes into the Akkusativ — and that is the next lesson.
Same word, different job. Look at these two English sentences:

The dog sees the man. → the dog is the subject (it is doing the seeing)
The man sees the dog. → the dog is the object (it is being seen)

Identical word, opposite job. English shows the difference by position. German shows it by changing the article. That is the whole idea, and lesson 61 is about exactly this.
